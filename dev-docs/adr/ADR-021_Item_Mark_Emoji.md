# ADR-021: One emoji per item as the packing row's scan aid — emoji vs. an icon library vs. photo-only

**Status:** Accepted (2026-08-22)
**Related:** PRD Addendum §3.28 (FR-28.1–28.11), UI-Spec G-15, ADR-002 (photo BLOBs outside the sync envelope), ADR-013
(visual baselines), ADR-014 (the tag initial tile), CLAUDE.md invariants 9/9b, `client/src/domain/itemMarks.ts`,
`client/src/theme/typography.css`

**Decision Drivers (in priority order):**
1. **Distinguishability at the size the row actually renders.** The scan aid is 22–34 px beside a name. A symbol that
   cannot be told from its neighbour at that size is decoration, not a scan aid.
2. **Coverage of a real packing list, including its tail.** Forty rows of household detail, not fifteen rows of travel
   gear. A system is decided by what it *cannot* say.
3. **The rules the app is built on** — one colour table (invariant 9), one type table, shape from the third (9b), and
   the G-11 role anchors.
4. **Footprint** (NFR-4.3) and **agreement between devices**: a packing list is shared (FR-4.x), so two people must see
   the same picture.
5. **The mark is never required.** Absence stays a first-class state; nothing else in the product may come to depend on
   it.

---

## Considered Options

The round was **rendered, not argued** (`dev-docs/UI_Concept_ItemMark_variants.html`, generated by
`build-item-mark-variants.mjs`): four marks against one fifteen-row list in one frame, seeded on purpose with a row
nothing fits (*Trekkingstöcke*) and several near-misses (*Fleecepullover*, *Schlafsack*, *Wasserflasche* — Unicode has
no water bottle).

### Option A — One optional emoji per item and per template *(recommended, accepted)*

`items.icon` / `templates.icon`, chosen from a curated ~100-entry index with German and English keywords, suggested from
the item's name, rendered from a self-hosted subsetted face.

**Pros**
- **Distinguishable at 22 px**, because emoji are coloured and shaped rather than stroked. This is the driver the round
  was run to settle, and it is the one Option B loses.
- **Covers the tail better**: 6 substitutions in 15 rows against the icon library's 7. That margin is one row and proves
  little on its own; what it does show is that the library's coverage is not the compensation for its
  distinguishability.
- The suggestion is **cheap and offline** — one keyword index, scored client-side, identical in all three modes
  (invariant 4, FR-28.11). Search and suggestion read the same list, so they cannot drift.
- Optional by construction. `NULL` is the default, and FR-28.1 makes "no mark" an ordinary row rather than a gap.

**Cons**
- **The mark is the one surface whose colours do not come from the token table.** A deliberate hole in invariant 9. G-15
  is what keeps it from spreading: the mark is content — never a button, a tab, a heading, a status pill, a progress bar
  or a substitute for a tag colour.
- **A font subset in the bundle**: 80 KB of woff2 for 103 code points, beside ~180 KB for the two text faces. Measured
  before it was committed.
- **Adding the face rewrites every visual baseline** (ADR-013) — one deliberate `make visual-update`, in the
  implementing PR.
- Curation is now a maintained artifact: the index, the subset and the CSS `unicode-range` are one decision expressed in
  three files, held together by `scripts/mark-font-gate.mjs`.

### Option B — An icon library (monochrome strokes in a role colour)

**Pros**
- **The only option that fits every rule without an exception**: one hue from the role anchors, one stroke weight, sized
  from `--jp-icon-*`. Nothing about it needs a carve-out in invariant 9.
- No font subset, no `unicode-range`, no curated index to maintain — the icon set ships with the icon set.
- Renders identically everywhere by construction.

**Cons**
- **It lost on the pixels.** At 34 px, sunscreen, bottle and water bottle are the same stroke. That is the decision, and
  it is not recoverable by better curation.
- Its substitution rate was the **higher** of the two (7 of 15): libraries carry travel gear and not household detail,
  which is most of a real packing list.
- The suggestion would have to be built anyway, against a list with worse coverage.

### Option C — Photo only (do nothing; §3.22 as it stands)

**Pros**
- Zero new anything: no column, no font, no picker, no rule exception, no baselines.
- The photo is the more *specific* answer where it exists — it says **which** jacket.

**Cons**
- **It does not answer the question that was asked.** Nobody photographs forty items; the photo exists on a handful of
  rows, so a forty-row list still has nothing left of the name.
- Rendered on its own it showed the cost honestly: three photographed rows out of fifteen **dominate** the column and
  read as more important than the twelve beside them.

### Option D — A coloured initial tile (the honest null variant)

**Pros**
- No new data, no font, no picker. Every row is covered, by construction.

**Cons**
- **Worse than no mark.** It repeats, in a coloured circle, the name standing right next to it — colour without meaning
  is noise. This is why the M4/M5 ladder deliberately ends in *nothing* rather than in a letter (FR-28.4), even though
  ADR-014's tile already exists on M9, where the column is identification rather than scanning.

## Decision Matrix

Scores 1–5, higher is better.

| Driver | Weight | A — emoji | B — icon library | C — photo only | D — initial |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Distinguishable at 22–34 px | 5 | **5** | 2 — same stroke | 4 — where one exists | 1 — repeats the name |
| Covers a real list's tail | 4 | **4** — 6/15 substituted | 3 — 7/15 | 1 — a handful of rows | 5 — but says nothing |
| Fits the token-table rules | 4 | 2 — one written exception | **5** | **5** | 4 |
| Footprint & device agreement | 3 | 3 — 80 KB, self-hosted | **5** | **5** | **5** |
| Optional, nothing depends on it | 3 | **5** | **5** | **5** | 2 — every row gets one |
| **Total** | | **73** | 70 | 64 | 58 |

The two leaders are one point apart, and the matrix is not what decided it: **driver 1 is the reason the feature
exists**, and B fails it in a way no weighting repairs. C and D are recorded because both were rendered and both taught
something that is now in the spec — C that a photo-only column reads as a hierarchy nobody chose, D that a mark which
merely restates the name is worse than none.

---

## Decision

Each master item and each template carries **one optional emoji** in an ordinary nullable, synced column (`items.icon`,
`templates.icon`, `CHECK (length(icon) <= 32)`). It is chosen from a curated, searchable index of packing-relevant
entries carrying German and English keywords, suggested from the row's own name and never applied without a tap. It
renders through **one shared component** owning the per-surface fallback ladder (M9: photo → mark → tag initial; M4/M5:
photo → mark → empty slot; templates: mark → nothing), from a **self-hosted subsetted emoji face** served by the
instance.

The length cap is the server's **only** validation. Unicode adds emoji every year, so a table of "real" emoji would
silently reject next year's valid input on a field where a wrong value costs a wrong little picture (FR-28.9). Curation
lives in the client, which is also where the picker is.

## Consequences

**Positive**
- A forty-row list gets a scan aid at the cost of a single tap per item, on the item rather than on each trip: FR-28.7
  makes the trip row *inherit* the mark through `source_item_id`, so one edit reaches every trip including archived
  ones, and there is no per-trip drift to reconcile.
- `templates.icon` retires the product's oldest fake: 📷 *Makro Fotografie* and ⛺ *Camping Basis* have been hardcoded in
  the prototype since §3.27, and M7, M3 step 3, M8's group section and the FR-27.12 peek sheet all had a slot drawn for
  a field that did not exist.
- The picker and the suggestion work with no server present (invariant 4), so nothing degrades in Local Mode.

**Negative / accepted costs**
- **A deliberate hole in invariant 9.** The mark's colours come from the font, not from `catppuccin.css`. Two things
  contain it: G-15 confines the mark to content, and a unit gate (`markRendering.spec.ts`) fails the build if any view
  outside the two mark components applies the mark face or renders an `icon` value — the same shape as the FR-21.7
  hex-in-`client/src` rule that keeps colours in one table.
- **80 KB of woff2**, and a third artifact to keep in step: the curated index, the subset and the CSS `unicode-range`
  are one decision in three files. `scripts/mark-font-gate.mjs` compares them and names both directions of the drift.
- **The baseline cost was budgeted and did not arrive.** §3.28 and this ADR's first draft both said a self-hosted face
  rewrites *every* visual baseline (ADR-013). The deliberate `make visual-update` moved **four of twenty-two**, all M4 —
  and not because of the face at all: the fixture's rows are ad-hoc and carry no marks, so no emoji is painted anywhere
  in the suite. What moved was the **held empty slot**, which shifts the names 32 px right. The prediction was wrong for
  a reason worth keeping: a face confined to *content* (G-15) is invisible to every screen that has no content of that
  kind, which is exactly the containment the exception was granted for.
- **The schema change destroys every development database**, including the maintainer's `:3000` instance (invariant 2 /
  ADR-018). Reseed with the M2 dev button.

**Neutral**
- Emoji rendering differs between platforms, which is normally an argument against them — here it is the argument *for*
  self-hosting: a packing list is shared, and on platform faces the sender and the reader would look at different
  pictures of the same row. Measured on the pinned Playwright image: 🧥 is a tan trench coat in the self-hosted face and
  a navy peacoat on both platform faces.
- `trip_items` gains no column, so the sync envelope grows by one short text field on two master tables and nothing
  else. This is deliberately *not* the ADR-002 treatment — that split exists because BLOBs bloat every device's pull,
  and a mark is a handful of bytes.

## Revisit Trigger

**Someone wanting a different mark for the same item on one particular trip** — that is the assumption FR-28.7 rests on,
and the one that would force a `trip_items` column and a screen to reconcile the two. Secondarily: if the curated index
has to grow past roughly 250 entries to stop feeling narrow, the subset stops being cheap and Option B's economics are
worth re-rendering — at that point, at the sizes the app renders by then.
