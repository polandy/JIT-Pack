# 3.9 Trip Feedback & Post-Trip Review

* **FR-9.3 (Capturing Trip Feedback Without Visiting Every Row, *built*):**
  An active trip is editable — adding something bought on the way, so that the next trip or
  template accounts for it, and marking packing elements as unused. The asymmetry between the
  two halves is the requirement.

  ***Missing* costs nothing.** It is stamped by the act itself: anything typed into the M4
  quick-add on an active trip is flagged (FR-5.6), so the user buys a travel adapter, adds it
  to the list because they want it packed, and the feedback is a by-product of a thing they
  wanted to do anyway. ***Unused*, through M5 alone, costs three taps per row and a decision to
  go looking**: open the row's sheet, unfold *Details* — a block whose subtitle reads *„Wer ·
  Beschaffung · Gepäck · Flags"* — and set a toggle. Nothing there asks for it, and twenty
  overpacked things are sixty taps at the moment the user is coming home. **And an
  `active`-only window would shut before the consequence is visible**: FR-9.2's assistant runs
  on the *archived* trip, so the first time anyone sees what a flag was worth, it could no
  longer be given or taken back.

  This matters more than a convenience: *unused* is the input FR-9.2 is built around
  (overpacking), and FR-14.3's long-term trends read the same column. A flag nobody sets is
  an assistant with nothing to assist and a trend line over an empty set.

  **The decision, three parts:**

  * **The judgement leaves the fold.** *ungenutzt* joins the row's **press-and-hold menu** in
    M4, next to FR-5.5's *„Nicht einpacken"* — the idiom this app already uses for a one-word
    judgement about a row, and one gesture from the list instead of three taps into a sheet.
    The M5 *Details* toggles stay as the spelled-out home, exactly the menu-plus-control pair
    FR-5.5 settled on (variants A + C there).
  * **A closing pass at the moment of archiving.** *„Reise abschliessen"* does not archive
    straight away: it puts **M4 into a review posture** listing the rows that went along
    and the FR-5.5-skipped rows that stayed home, each with a verdict (FR-9.6), and a way to finish without judging
    anything. This is where going through the trip once at the end lives, and it is the only
    point in the lifecycle where the user is thinking about the whole trip at once. A row neither packed nor skipped
    has no outcome to judge and is left out. The pass is skippable and
    produces no flags when skipped; it must never become a gate in front of archiving. **Its
    door is M2's** (a ⋮ holds its own context, and the trip's lifecycle steps are the trip's,
    not the packing's): *„Reise abschliessen"* on the trip's row or hero opens M4 in the pass
    (`?closing=1`), and M2 offers no archive that goes round it.
  * **The window stays open on the archived trip.** FR-9.1's `active`-only gate does not apply
    to *unused*: the flag stays settable and revocable on an archived trip, because that is where M14 shows what it did.
    *Missing* keeps its automatic stamp; afterwards it is set only as FR-9.6's verdict on a row that stayed home — a
    thing bought after the trip is not a thing that was missing on it, but a thing left at home and then needed is.
    That the flags „only mean anything on a live trip" is true of *setting* them in the moment and false of
    *correcting* them, and the correction is the half the assistant makes visible.

  *Considered and rejected:* **a multi-select mode on M4** — a second selection model on the
  busiest screen of the app, carried year-round for a job done once per trip, and it would
  still have to be discovered; **inferring *unused* from behaviour** (packed but never
  touched) — the app cannot see use, and guessing here would feed FR-9.2 a fabricated opinion
  that the user then has to argue with; **asking one row at a time in a card stack** — FR-27.11
  rejected the stack for the same harvest, for the same reason: it hides how much is left.

  **Where the pass lives:** it is **a mode of M4 that archives and continues to M14**. The
  rendered pair (`UI_Concept_ClosingPass_variants.html` at `6b148419`, built by
  `build-closing-pass-variants.mjs` at `6b148419` with the prototype's stylesheet lifted verbatim, so no
  difference between the options can come from the CSS) set its own screen against a mode of
  M4, and neither wins outright — the chosen shape is a **third one**.

  **Why neither of the two:** a mode of M4 wins the question you ask first — at a hundred and
  twenty rows it brings the grouping, the FR-25.11 facets and the search, and none of that has
  to be built a second time — and loses the question you ask last, because it ends where it
  began, in the packing list of a trip that no longer exists. Its own screen is the mirror
  image: an endless single column, whose one virtue is that it has an **exit**, and the exit
  leads where the marks are going. So the mode keeps M4's rendering and takes the screen's
  ending: *„Fertig"* **archives the trip and opens M14**, rather than merely switching a mode
  off. A closing pass that hands you back the list you just finished with is not a closing
  pass.

  **What the choice costs, and the rule that pays it:** M4's row carries a press-and-hold
  (FR-5.5, and this FR adds *ungenutzt* to that same menu). **A screen asking one question
  offers nothing that answers another.** In the review posture the row has exactly one gesture
  — the tap, which opens the row's verdict sheet (FR-9.6) — and press-and-hold is inert; the two menu entries are
  both reachable a second earlier, on the same rows, before the pass is entered. The posture also drops the
  quick-add row, the ＋ FAB, the FR-25.2 *„{n} Erledigte anzeigen"* reveal bar and the app-bar
  cluster's *Reise bearbeiten* and *Reise abschliessen* — the archive action, which would be
  offered from inside the room it opens. What stays is what the mode was chosen for (grouping,
  FR-25.11 facets, search), plus one line under the list that adds a row the list never had (FR-9.6).
  **The verdict is a control of its own**: a chip rendering straight off the row's flags and
  `needed_quantity`, not a checkbox — that is M4's *packed* idiom, sitting beside rows whose
  subtitle reads „gepackt · heute", and an Ionic-owned checked state drifts from the row. G-3's
  padlock still wins over it.

  *Also rejected:* making the pass its own M-number for the sake of being unenterable by
  accident. It cannot be walked into either way — the only door is the archive action, and it
  is a door that asks.

  **Revisit trigger:** the pass is skipped every time. If it is, the pass asks the wrong
  question at the wrong moment, and the answer is not to make it harder to skip.

* **FR-9.4 (M14 With Real Proposals, *built*):** how the review assistant (FR-9.2) behaves with
  populated cards. The dev fixture (`reviewFixture.ts`) renders that state.

  * ***„Nie mehr fragen" is a worded button*, not an unlabelled ✕** — `aria-label` and `title`
    are no label at all on a phone, and it is the one consequential action on the screen:
    device-local and permanent. It follows FR-27.15's answer for the same kind of dismissal — a
    worded *„Ignorieren"* button, and *„Rückgängig"* on its sibling path — rather than a second
    grammar for „ask me no more".
  * **A handled card leaves the *Offen* block.** Handled rows stay visible rather than vanish
    (FR-27.11), under the outcome block, and *Offen* holds only what is open — so the heading's
    count and the cards under it agree, and nothing is counted in two blocks.
  * **The empty state is reachable by handling the proposals**: *„Nichts zu prüfen"* appears
    once nothing is open, not only after every proposal has been dismissed permanently.
  * **A handled proposal is one record line under *Erledigt***: kind, item, target group,
    outcome. The same card at reduced opacity would keep a target picker, a peek chevron and a
    blast-radius line for a decision already made. The block is a record of the pass, not a
    second workspace, which is what lets it hold twenty rows without becoming the screen.
  * **The snackbar never lands on the navigation bar.** Ionic's `positionAnchor` puts a bottom
    toast above a named element; deciding the anchor per screen makes it forgettable, so the
    decision lives in `client/src/composables/shared/toast.ts` and every call site goes through it: a caller's
    own anchor still wins, and **a hidden tab bar counts as no tab bar** — Ionic measures a
    `display: none` anchor as a zeroed box and subtracts a whole viewport height from the
    offset, which throws the toast off screen instead of onto the bar. E2E-M22-09 asserts the
    geometry.

* **FR-9.5 (The Forgotten Radar on a Planning Trip, *built*):** what an earlier, similar trip recorded as *Missing*
  is shown on the trip being planned, so the same gap is not left open twice. FR-9.2 folds a missing item back into a
  group, but only where somebody runs the review and applies it; the radar reads the record itself, at the moment it
  is worth most — while the next list is still being built.

  * **The reference trip.** The newest **archived** trip of the **same series** (FR-13.1); where the trip has no
    series, or its series has no archived trip yet, the newest archived trip that shares a **Ferien-Vorlage** with it
    (`trip_template_sources`, kind `template`) — a shared **group** is not enough, because a camera group travels to the
    beach and to the mountains alike. Newest by year, then start date, an undated trip after a dated one in the same
    year.
    **One trip, named**, never a merge of several: the card has to say where its claim comes from, and a list pooled
    over five years would carry a gap that was closed four years ago. Tags were weighed and set aside — a trip carries
    none, and adding them would be a schema and a wizard step for a question the series already answers.
  * **The entries.** Every row of the reference trip flagged *Missing* (FR-9.1) — both a row bought on the way
    (*„Fehlte auf der Liste"*) and FR-5.11's *Vergessen*, a skipped row that stayed home (*„Vergessen einzupacken"*,
    the same words the revealed row uses). Each entry carries the **comments written on that row** (FR-7.1), newest
    first, FR-27.9's slice taken by **row id** rather than by `source_item_id`: the entry *is* those rows, so a row
    with no inventory item keeps its remarks too, and the author line follows FR-27.9's rule — absent where nobody can
    be named.
  * **An entry leaves once the item is on the list.** Derived from the rows, nothing stored: a row of the planning
    trip with the same `source_item_id`, or — for an entry or row without one — the same name, case-insensitively.
    Name matching is right here where FR-27.9 refuses it, because the failure points the other way: there a wrong
    match puts a stranger's remark on an item, here a missed match nags about something already packed. It works
    alike for an entry added from the card and one added any other way, which is why the card needs no memory of its
    own. With nothing left the card is **absent**, not empty.
  * ***„Auf die Liste"*** adds one **trip-global** row from the inventory item (the M14 harvest's shape, E2E-FLOW-04),
    with FR-20.4's companions, unflagged: the trip is planning, and the plan did not forget it this time.
  * ***„Ausblenden"*** hides the card for this trip, **device-local** (`localStorage`), like M14's *„Nie mehr fragen"*:
    a muting rather than domain data, and the worst case is another device showing it once more.
  * **Only on a planning trip.** Once the trip is active the time to act on a packing list's head is over, and an
    archived trip has its own closing card.
  * **Modes (invariant 5).** Computed client-side over the trips the device holds (invariant 4). **Local** and
    **Single-User**: the card as described, with no author on a comment. **Server**: authors are named; the reference
    trip's rows are fetched the way M2's ring fetches them (`ensureTripData`, ADR-033), and until they have arrived —
    offline, say — the card names the trip and says its list is not on this device yet, rather than saying nothing,
    which would read as *nothing was forgotten*.

* **FR-9.6 (Three Verdicts and a Reason, *built*):** a trip is judged afterwards row by row, in three verdicts, each
  with an optional free-text reason. FR-9.1's two flags answered *whether* a thing earned its place, never *how
  many*, and a reason had nowhere to be written beside the judgement — so the review proposed blind, and the next
  planning trip could not say why.

  * **The three verdicts.** *„Hätte mit gemusst"* — a skipped row that was needed — is `flag_missing`. *„Unnötig
    dabei"* — a row taken along and never used — is `flag_unused`. *„Zu viel"* — a packed row of which fewer would
    have done — is **`trip_items.needed_quantity`**, the number actually used, from 1 to one less than `packed_count`;
    NULL is no verdict. What a row is offered follows from what happened to it: any row not skipped takes *unnötig*
    (a row nobody ticked may still have gone along), *zu viel* once two or more were packed; a skipped row takes
    *hätte mit gemusst*. A row carries **one verdict**:
    setting one clears the other two. Two devices judging one row differently offline can still leave two, because
    setting a flag merges additively; M14 then shows both proposals and either applies cleanly.
  * **Why a column for *zu viel*** (ADR-103): a quantity proposal needs a target, and nothing on the row holds one —
    `quantity` is the plan, `packed_count` what went along. Lowering by one would be the app guessing.
  * **The reason is an ordinary row comment** (FR-7.1, `comments`), written by the verdict sheet's *„Warum?"* field,
    its author stamped as for any comment. FR-27.9's *„Kommentare aus Reisen"* in M10 and FR-9.5's radar read it with
    no change, and changing the verdict later leaves what was said standing in the row's thread.
  * **Where it is set.** In FR-9.3's closing pass — every row that went along or stayed home, a tap opening the
    verdict sheet — and in M5's *Details* on an active or archived trip, where the verdict block replaces the two flag
    toggles. A planning trip offers none. The pass ends with *„+ Fehlte, stand gar nicht auf der Liste"*: FR-5.11's
    *Vergessen* add (a skipped row at quantity 0 with `flag_missing`), so a thing that was never on the list is judged
    in the same sitting. M4's press-and-hold *ungenutzt* stays as the one-gesture path.
  * **What it proposes (FR-9.2, extending FR-27.11).** A verdict never writes to a group; M14 does, on *Übernehmen*.
    *unnötig* and *hätte mit gemusst* keep their proposals (*unused*, *missing*). *zu viel* adds a third kind,
    **fewer**: the group line the row came from is lowered by the surplus (`packed_count − needed_quantity`), never
    below 1 — zero is *unused*'s proposal — and not proposed where the line is already at or below the result. Several
    rows of one item from one group (one per traveler) propose the highest of their results, so nobody is cut below
    what they used. A skipped row from a group that still holds the item yields no *missing* proposal — the group
    already brings it; the radar (FR-9.5) carries it to the next planning trip. Each proposal quotes the comments on
    its rows under its why line, newest first, with FR-27.9's author rule.
  * **Modes (invariant 5).** The verdict, the number and the reason are row data, and the proposals are computed on
    the device (invariant 4), so **Server**, **Single-User** and **Local** judge and review alike. Server names a
    reason's author and syncs it to everyone on the trip; Single-User and Local leave the author out (G-8); Local needs
    no network. No endpoint, no mode gate.
