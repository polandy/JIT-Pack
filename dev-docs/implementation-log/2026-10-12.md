## A hang that was not load (2026-10-13)

Stress runs (`--workers=8` on the planner specs, the `server` project beside a CPU burner) failed six times in
`createTripViaWizard`: the step-1 name field never came, or the head stayed on *New trip* after *Create trip*. The
premise was load. It was not: 448 loaded planner runs passed, and a run with **no** load but a loop creating and
removing a docker network every two seconds failed 2 of 28 with exactly both symptoms.

The traces said why. Three milliseconds after `goto /trips/new`, the entry's own static chunks failed with
`net::ERR_NETWORK_CHANGED` and the module graph never ran — a blank page. After *Create trip*, M4's lazy chunks failed
the same way and the page error was *Failed to fetch dynamically imported module: PackingListPage*, so
`router.replace` was rejected and the wizard stayed. Chromium cancels every request in flight when its network-change
notifier fires; `scripts/e2e.sh` and `scripts/visual.sh` ran the container with `--network host`, so that notifier
watched the host's 28 interfaces, and every container another project on the machine started or stopped was a change.
Load only lengthened the window in which a request was in flight. 19 of the 25 failures of a loaded `server` run
carried the same error.

The containers now keep their own network namespace (`lo` and `eth0`, nothing for a neighbour to change). Every server
the suite drives already ran inside beside the browser, so nothing needed the host's ports — which also ends the
two-worktree port collision the `E2E_*_PORT` forwarding existed for, so that went. The same churn on the fix: 84 of
84 green.

**Rejected:** catching `vite:preloadError` and reloading, or retrying the import. It would cover the lazy-route half
only — a static chunk lost at boot leaves no app running to retry anything — and it would make the suite pass over an
environment that cancels requests rather than remove the cause. Whether the installed app should recover from a chunk
lost to a real network switch (a phone leaving Wi-Fi) is a product question of its own, not this flake's fix.

## A verdict needs its head (2026-10-17)

E2E-G10-02 lets Bob fall behind, lifts the block and waits for Alice's socket to report everyone in sync again. It
flaked three times (runs 34163387665, 35289725004, 37764993790). #808 narrowed the wait to a frame of this trip that
lists both people. The premise was that a stale frame would differ in one of those. It does not. Bob can catch up to
the *second* item before Alice adds the third, and that frame names the same trip, both people and everyone at the
head. Only the head itself had moved, and the frame did not say which head it meant. The TODO's suggested fix (wait
for the third item's cursor) had nothing to match against, because a presence frame carried no cursor.

The rejected option was to accept only an in-sync frame that arrives after the third item's `trip.changed` on Alice's
socket. That needs no wire change, but `broadcastPresence` reads the head and only then queues the frame. A frame
judged against the old head can therefore still be queued behind the new `trip.changed`: a narrow window, but it is
still open. The presence payload now carries `head_seq`, the head its verdicts were judged against (Sync-API §7). The
case takes the third item's head from that `trip.changed` and accepts only a frame at that head or later. A logged run
showed one `trip.changed` per add (heads 4 → 8 → 10) and Bob's in-sync frame at 10.

## Dates that read today (2026-10-17)

E2E-M27-14 went red on its own on 14 October: its excursion's last day passed and folded behind the past toggle
(FR-31.10). The premise of the follow-up sweep was that the other specs with fixed dates and no `setClock` (the planner
specs, `helpers/m4.ts`, `helpers/trips.ts`) were the same kind of case. They were not. Every fixed-date case was traced
to the rules that read today: the day counter, `hasDeparted`/`pastPacking`, the opening day of the plan, the
excursion fold and the date pickers' bounds. Each case either has no start date, gets its phase from an explicit
*Start*/*Archive*, or asserts nothing those rules decide. The wizard and *Trip properties* set no `min`/`max`, and
the trip list's segments follow status, never dates.

Two traps were found that are not on screen. `followsGroups` drops a trip whose end date is gone, so its groups
no longer offer changes and FR-27.4 does nothing. `M4_TRIP` (~50 cases) and `PER_PERSON_TRIP` ended on 2026-12-31,
which was harmless only because none of those cases includes a group. The second trap is the range picker. It lists
months from 12 before today to 24 after, and `revealMonth` allows 10 loads of 12 months. A fixed date therefore costs
one extra tap per year that passes, and it is out of reach after about eleven years. That is slow but not red, so it
was left alone. The owner chose to drop the fixture dates (an open-ended trip behaves the same forever) rather than pin
~50 cases to a clock or move the date to 2099, which would mean six *later* taps per wizard.

**Rejected:** a gate refusing a date literal in a case without `setClock`. Around 60 cases would trip it and only one
ever depended on today, so each would need a clock it does not need or an opt-out comment, and the gate would mostly
produce noise. Instead, the README's clock rule names the two hidden readers of today (owner's call).
