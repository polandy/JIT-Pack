## A save lost under a moving card (2026-10-06)

E2E-M27-18 went red once on `main`'s WebKit leg: *Save as way back* was clicked and the sheet stayed open.

**A premise that was wrong: a click Playwright calls done reached its target.** The trace's action snapshot had the
connection card in the page at `expand-enter-active expand-enter-to`, growing above the save button. Vue holds an
entering element at `-enter-from` for a double animation frame before it moves, so Playwright saw two still frames and
called the button stable; its hit-target check covers a press's first event only (`result` is computed once in
`setupHitTargetInterceptor`), so the mousedown met the button, a frame landed before the mouseup on the
three-frames-a-second runner, the button was ~130 px lower, and the browser sent the click to the common ancestor.
Neither a throttled `requestAnimationFrame` nor CPU throttling reproduced it, because WebKit advances a transition only
at a rendering update and locally none fell between the two events; flushing two frames at mousedown with reduced
motion on reproduced it 10 of 10 in both engines — at the *first* save too, after the case had already waited for the
card's text, which is there from the enter's first frame.

**A second wrong premise, in the fix: a flag cleared on `after-leave`.** Vue's out-in calls the leave's `remove()` —
which renders the entering element and fires its `before-enter` — before the user's `onAfterLeave`, so the flag read
"settled" for the frames the card had not yet moved. `useTransitionSettled` counts instead, and its spec steps frames by
hand and was red against the flag.

**An option rejected: keeping the save button still.** Taking the connection without the add row's collapse, or
pinning the actions to the sheet's kept height, removes one movement but not the enter's still frames, so a case would
still need a signal; and the double movement is not one a finger meets in practice. The cost: a case pressing anything
the card pushes must wait for `data-settled` (`connectionTaken`), which the e2e README now names.

## The who column became the lead slot (2026-10-06)

UX-03 measured M4 at 412 px on the sample trip: the for-whom seat (FR-25.28) stood beside the mark on every row, so
names started at x 101 and *Kleidung*'s name columns were 175–198 px. Three variants were rendered on the live seed
(`.claude/mockups/mockup-ux03-row-lead.html`, untracked) and the owner chose the one lead slot.

**An option rejected: the seat column only in groups that need it** (the review's own suggestion). It keeps the slot
where a group holds a face and drops it elsewhere, so *Bad* and *Küche* start their names at 69 and *Aktivität* and
*Technik* at 101 — on one screen, which is the FR-21.19 break the lead column exists to prevent.

**An option deferred: an overlapping facepile on a shut head** (+12 px on a three-person head). It is the row's other
edge, which UX-03 only mentions; it would be its own decision.

**A cost accepted: the door no longer looks like one.** The mark or face is now the seat, and a mark does not invite a
tap the way the dashed people glyph did — which is why *Für wen …* went into the row's and the head's press-and-hold
menus at the same time. A shared row without a mark has an empty slot that still opens the strip.

## One card, one element list (2026-10-07)

UX-06 found M2's hero a third shape beside M1's card, though both drew `TripHero`: each screen computed the props on
its own, and M2 had simply never passed the phase word, the day counter or the open count. Nothing failed — an
optional prop left out is not an error. `useTripHero` now words the trip for both, so a line is on both cards or on
neither; the screens keep only what they add below (M1's preview and day blocks, M2's chips).

**Rejected: a labelled *Packliste öffnen* at the card's foot.** It was the mockup's variant B. The whole card already
opens the packing list, so the foot would be a second link to the same place, 64 px tall, reading as the card's only
action. M1 has that foot on the road because there the card is *not* a link (FR-7.10) — the reason does not carry.

**Rejected: the ⋮ on every pointer.** On touch the hold is the menu on every list, and a glyph would be a second
surface for the same words — the thing the item removed. The ⋮ shows on `(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)` only, as
a sibling of the card rather than inside it, since the card is a link. The cost: the mobile visual baselines run under
Desktop Chrome and therefore show it.

**How the touch half is tested.** `page.emulateMedia` cannot set `pointer`, and a fresh `hasTouch` context would need
the whole seed again. CDP's `Emulation.setTouchEmulationEnabled` flips `(pointer: fine)` to false in the running page
(measured), so E2E-M2-35b sees the ⋮ with a mouse and then without — Chromium only, WebKit has no CDP.

## M4's door for M6 and M25 (2026-10-07)

UX-07 named four screens with an always-open composer and a FAB that only focused it. Rendered at 412 and 360 px,
**the premise held for two**: M6 and M25 share `ListComposer`, a 91 px card that took the first row of both lists,
with a FAB that scrolled up and focused it — and that kept floating over the list with the keyboard up, covering the
one row still visible at 360 px. M31 and M29 have no composer: their FAB opens a sheet of their own. M4 and M8 had
long had the answer (FR-21.24, `QuickAddItem`); M6 and M25 were the outliers. The owner chose from a rendered round of
four variants.

**Rejected: the composer stays and the FAB goes** (variant B, with the card shrunk to its field row at rest). It saves
the FAB but puts adding behind a scroll to the top of the longest lists — what FR-21.24 rejected for M4 — and would
make M6/M25 feel unlike M4/M8. **Rejected: a dashed invitation plus the FAB** (C, the review's own suggestion): two
doors to one composer, against FR-21.24. **Rejected: a composer on M29/M31** — an entry carries a day, a time and for
whom, a meal a slot, a day and its ingredients; one line on top of the list holds none of that.

**A trap: an empty list that is only not loaded yet.** The composer opens by itself on an empty list (G-7), and the
watch only ever opens — a list that fills is the run the composer is there for. So M6 passes
`rowsLoaded && nothingAtAll`: before the rows arrive `nothingAtAll` is true, and without the guard every visit would
open the composer and leave it open. M25's composer is mounted only once the trip is loaded, so its `noTasks` needs
no guard. Unlike M4, opening focuses the field: FR-25.13c's reason — suggestion chips the keyboard would cover —
does not exist here.

**A trap: Ionic names a FAB once.** `ion-fab-button` copies `aria-label` onto its inner button when it loads and never
again, so M29's FAB went on announcing the day it was first drawn on after the strip chose another. E2E-M29-21 found
it; the button is now keyed on the chosen day, so a new day is a new button with its own name.

## Every chip whole (2026-10-07)

UX-08 found three rows in M31's meal sheet scrolling with hard edges and *Gemeinsam* cut to *Gemeinsa…* on the
for-whom line. Measured on the reference device, the line was **one pixel short**: every toggle took an equal share
(`flex: 1 0 40px`), 67 px, and *Gemeinsam* needs 68 — while the whole line needs 252 of its 360. Each toggle now takes
its word's width with the 40 px floor and shares out the rest; a roster whose names do not fit scrolls. The owner chose
from an interactive mockup (`.claude/mockups/mockup-ux08-chip-rows-live.html`).

**Rejected: *Gemein-/sam* on two lines** — whole, but the line grows 18 px and the break reads odd. **Rejected:
*Geteilt*** — it fits, but *Gemeinsam* is the model's word everywhere else. **For the slots, the owner chose short
words over a wrapped second row or a 2 × 2 grid**: *Morgen · Mittag · Znüni/Zvieri · Abend* became `meals.slotShort`,
which the chips now read. That key also labels M31's rows, the drag chip, M1's block and a shopping part, so *Früh*
and the cipher *Zw.* go there too; the long names stay the sheet's title, and M29's `slotWord` is UX-10's.

**One rule, lifted rather than copied.** UX-04 had drawn the switcher's fades, snap and centring inside
`TripViewNav.vue`. They are `useEdgeFades` with `edgeFades.css` now, used by the switcher, `ChipRow scroll` (the
trip's days, the earlier dishes) and the for-whom line; `ChipRow` otherwise wraps, and a chip wider than its row breaks
its words inside the chip. The meal sheet's `presented` flag existed only to delay a `scrollIntoView` until the sheet
was laid out; the composable's ResizeObserver centres when the row first gets a size, so the flag went.

**A trap: no browser breaks a word at a slash.** The new *Morgen* was wider than M31's 54 px label column and broke
inside the word, and *Znüni/Zvieri* had no break point at all — the drag chip's equal fields cut it to *Znüni/Zvi*. The
column now holds *Morgen* (60 px; M1's block the same), the label never breaks inside a word, and the catalogue's
*Znüni/Zvieri* carries a zero-width space after its slash: the one place it may break, invisible on a chip, which never
wraps its word. Found by rendering the list and the drag at 360 px, not by the cases.

## One shape per meaning on a day line (2026-10-07)

UX-09 found M29's trailing column saying three things with two round shapes: a ring (a share, read only) beside an
empty circle that ticked an idea *Gemacht* or resolved a task, and nothing on entries. The owner chose from an
interactive mockup (`.claude/mockups/mockup-ux09-dayline-trailing.html`): a task gets M25's `IonCheckbox`, an idea
nothing, the ring stays with a name by count (*„2 von 4 Zutaten"*, *„3 von 8 gepackt"*) and moves inside the line's
button, so a tap on it opens the line instead of doing nothing.

**Rejected: the idea keeping a tick, round or square.** It cost the one thing it did — *Gemacht* from the plan and M1's
*Heute* card in one tap; now it is the idea's own sheet on M28. Two tickable shapes meaning different writes was the
defect, and a checkbox on an idea would read as a task. `stateAfterTick` went with it. **Rejected: a bare arc** — the
number repeats the second line, but the ring then reads as decoration where the other rings in the app carry a figure.

**The time column was one pixel short already.** It was `flex: 0 0 44px` with the flex item's `min-width: auto`, so a
word wider than 44 px pushed the title out of line: *abends* is 45 px in Hanken Grotesk at 13 px, and the new
*ganztags* 52. It is 56 px now. The column's words moved out of the template into `dayLineTime` beside
`dayLineWords`: time, the source's word (a meal's slot), *ganztags* for an excursion, and an empty string rather than
the dash otherwise.
