## A stale verdict outran the right one (2026-10-19)

E2E-G10-02 still flaked after #817 named `head_seq` on the presence frame (run 39125844356): Alice's socket-level
tracker saw the all-caught-up frame arrive, matching the right head, yet the facepile it was meant to update never
showed it. The premise behind #817 was that the frame itself could be wrong or ambiguous; naming its head ruled that
out, but the frame Alice's app ended up holding was still the *earlier* one — because a second, later-arriving frame
is not the same thing as a later-finishing one.

`broadcastPresence` reads `headSeq` from the database outside the hub's lock, the same way `broadcastRoster` already
documented and guarded against for its own race. Two calls for the same trip — Bob's cursor settling on the second
item, then on the third — can overtake each other between the DB read and the send: the call triggered first (cursor
at item two) can still be the one whose read finishes second, so it sends *after* the correct, caught-up frame and
overwrites it on the wire. Nothing in `wsRouting.ts` or `useSyncOrchestrator.setPresence` checks ordering either —
whichever frame arrives last wins, unconditionally.

The fix is the same shape as `broadcastRoster`'s, narrowed to a trip: `Hub.presenceGen` numbers each trip's presence
snapshots at the point the DB read is triggered, not when it completes, and a peer is never queued a generation older
than one it already holds (`conn.presenceSent`). `TestHub_Presence_AnOlderSnapshotNeverArrivesLast_FR4_6` reproduces
the overtake directly — a parked `headSeq` call standing in for the slow read — and fails without the guard exactly as
E2E-G10-02 did: Alice's last presence for Bob reads *behind* even though the in-sync frame reached the wire first.

**Rejected:** matching on `head_seq` alone, client-side, and dropping an older-headed frame on arrival. It would hide
the symptom on the one field the test happens to check, but two frames at the *same* head can still overtake each
other (e.g. two cursor reports for the same item), so the race itself would remain — the guard belongs where the
reordering happens, not where it is observed.

## A settled title was not a settled page (2026-10-19)

E2E-M9-27 went red on its first item (run 39310913582, FR-1.9): `m10-assignee`'s `.select-text` resolved to two
elements. Committing a new item does a `router.replace` to its editor, and for the length of that transition the
outlet holds both pages at once — the create form, still painted, and the editor, `ion-page-invisible`. Both carry
`m10-assignee`, and `visiblePage` excludes only `ion-page-hidden`, so a scoped locator resolved twice.

The premise behind `commitNewItem` and `createItem` was that `header-title` turning to the new name, or
`m10-edit-head` reporting visible, meant the editor's page was the one on screen. Neither does: the title lives in
the frame, outside the outlet, and follows the route — not the transition; the edit head is on the incoming page
from its first frame, and `ion-page-invisible` is `opacity: 0`, which Playwright counts as visible regardless.

The fix is `pageSettled` (already in `helpers/page.ts`, written for a different defect — a back-button double
transition): it waits for exactly one painted, non-invisible `.ion-page`. `commitNewItem` now reads the edit head
only once that holds, and every site that committed a new item and then read or acted on its page inside the
outlet without an intervening hard navigation shared the same exposure, not only the one CI happened to catch —
`createMasterItem` (two specs chain it and then click `m10-add-dependency` on the second item with no navigation
between) and one direct site in `item-mark.spec.ts` (a `header-back` click right after commit, the same overlap
`pageSettled`'s own doc comment already names as the "unclickable FAB" failure). All three now go through
`commitNewItem`.

## Every tag, folded to two lines (2026-10-20)

The tag chooser (M10 and the FR-24.11 sheet) offered the first eight unassigned tags and a „N weitere per Suche" tail,
so a tag past the cap could only be found by knowing its name. The owner wanted to pick from every tag without
typing, while the cap's reason — a grown instance's tags pushing every item form down — still held.

Three shapes were mocked at 12, 40 and 80 tags, at 410 px and at desktop width. **All open** answers the wish most
literally and is the smallest diff, but at 80 tags it is fourteen lines of chips above weight and price. A **swipe
band** (three rows scrolling sideways) holds a fixed height, but never shows every tag at once and needs Shift+wheel on
a desktop. The owner chose the **fold**: two lines, then *„Alle N zeigen"*, open for the rest of the session once
opened. Its cost is a state the other two do not have, and a measurement.

The fold is measured from the rendered chips' top edges (`chipsWithinRows`), not a chip count: how many tags share a
line depends on their names and the width, which is exactly why the old count of eight was two lines on one instance
and four on another. Laying all chips out and cutting them back happens before the browser paints, so the unfolded
row is never seen. It is re-measured when the offers or the width change, and when the web font replaces its fallback.

The measurement must leave the toggle mounted. The first version reset the count to "all" to measure, which removed the
toggle for one tick, and watched an array-returning getter, which counts as changed on every re-run. Together they
swallowed a tap after typing a name: the mousedown blurs the name field, its settle re-renders the form, the toggle is
rebuilt between mousedown and mouseup, and Chrome dispatches no click. Only the e2e case's second form showed it.

## A note where the snackbar stood (2026-10-21)

Once a traveller's share is packed, the row can say what exactly went in (*„Lange Hose" → „Jeans"*). The owner chose
from a mockup: the note is offered **by the pack snackbar** (rather than a row menu or M5 alone), it can be written
**before packing** too (M5's *Packnotiz*), a **clone copies it**, and it is capped at **60 characters**. Every
traveller on the trip sees and edits every note; it syncs as one field-level LWW column like any other.

An Ionic toast cannot hold an input, so *Notiz* swaps the toast for a bar of the same look in the same place
(`PackNoteBar`), and the pack's undo stays armed across the swap — `openNoteBar` drops the toast without clearing the
undo, only closing the bar clears it. The bar saves on Enter and nowhere else: on a phone the keyboard folding away
blurs the field, and a save on blur would write half a word each time. M5's field, with nothing waiting on it, does write
on change.

M5's field first sat on the sheet's first level, under the skip toggle. CI's E2E-M5-25 caught what the eyeball did
not: one more field took the folded sheet from under 80 % of an 880 px screen to its 85 % ceiling, so unfolding
Details no longer grew it. Loosening the bound would have hidden that. The owner moved the field into Details; the
glance row now carries the note quoted, so a packed row still says what went in without unfolding.

The feature pushed four files over their size budget; the ratchet was met by moving code out (`PackNoteField`,
`PackNoteSlot`, an action group `packNotes` per ADR-098) rather than raising a budget.

## Three verdicts and a reason (2026-10-22)

A finished trip is judged row by row: *hätte mit gemusst*, *unnötig dabei*, *zu viel*, each with a *Warum?*. The owner
chose from a mockup: a verdict only **proposes** (M14 still applies it to a group), its reason is read back in M14, the
radar and M10, it is set in the **closing pass and in M5**, and all three modes behave alike. The reason is an ordinary
row comment, so the radar and M10 read it without a change; only M14 learned to quote it.

*Zu viel* is the one verdict FR-9.1's flags could not carry. A `flag_fewer` that stepped the line down by one would
have been wrong for every overshoot but one, and inferring it from `quantity − packed_count` reads a packing slip as a
judgement. So the row stores how many were actually used (`needed_quantity`, migration 027, ADR-103), and M14 lowers
the group's line by the surplus — the highest result winning across travelers, never below one.

*Missing* after the trip only makes sense on a row that stayed home, so the closing pass now lists skipped rows under
*Daheim geblieben* beside *Eingepackt*, and a row neither packed nor skipped is left out — it has no outcome. The
shared rule lives in `domain/verdict.ts` (`passSection`, `verdictChoices`), which M4, M5 and the action read alike.
In M5 a chip outside its window stays visible but disabled (owner's call), so *not now* does not read as *no such
verdict*. One verdict per row is held by the write, not the merge: two offline edits can leave two, and M14 then
shows both.

Wiring the undo, the first version armed it without announcing the act, so the *verdict saved* toast never showed and
E2E-M4-123 timed out on its *Rückgängig* — every arm site pairs the two (`actUndoably`).
